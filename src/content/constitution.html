PROPOSED DRAFT (v0.2). This draft has not been adopted. It sets out the proposed constitution of a political party and must be reviewed by qualified Nigerian legal practitioners, adopted by the inaugural National General Assembly and registered with the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) before it takes effect. Figures shown in [square brackets] are proposals for the General Assembly to settle. PGC Nigeria is not yet a registered political party.

Preamble

We, citizens of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, believing that democratic participation does not end at the polling unit, and that public office is a mandate to deliver agreed development objectives, resolved to organise peacefully and lawfully as a political party whose every candidate seeks office on predetermined, published and verifiable development commitments to the people they would represent, adopt this Constitution.

Article 1 — Name, Status and Identity

  1. The party shall be known as The People's Governance Compact (in this Constitution, "PGC Nigeria", "the Party" or "the Compact").
  2. Its motto is "Democracy Beyond the Ballot" and its ideology is Governance by Objective, as set out in Article 4 and Article 18.
  3. PGC Nigeria shall apply to INEC for registration as a political party in accordance with sections 222 to 229 of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended) and the Electoral Act. Until registered, it shall not present itself as a registered political party, shall not sponsor candidates, and shall not hold primaries.
  4. PGC Nigeria is a political party. It shall not be a government agency, and its name, motto, emblem and logo shall carry no ethnic or religious connotation.
  5. Its headquarters shall be in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

Article 2 — Vision, Mission and Philosophy

  1. Vision: a Nigeria where citizens actively participate in governance and every elected public official is continuously accountable for measurable development outcomes.
  2. Mission: to organise, empower and equip Nigerian citizens with the information, structures and tools necessary to define development priorities, monitor public commitments and demand transparent, responsive and accountable governance.
  3. Philosophy: citizens retain the right and responsibility to follow government programmes, projects, expenditure and service delivery throughout the tenure of elected officials, through peaceful, constitutional and lawful means.

Article 3 — Objectives

The objectives of PGC Nigeria are to:

  1. facilitate community needs assessment and prioritisation in every ward, local government area, Area Council and state;
  2. formulate measurable community development compacts, and require every candidate sponsored by the Party to adopt predetermined development projects from them as a Governance Mandate;
  3. document public projects and monitor their funding and implementation using verifiable evidence;
  4. publish quarterly, evidence-based assessments of elected offices against their lawful responsibilities, with a right of reply;
  5. sponsor qualified, screened and mandate-bound candidates for elective public office at every level, and support them in lawful delivery;
  6. promote civic education, freedom of information and lawful citizen engagement with oversight institutions;
  7. apply the same standards of transparency and accountability to its own officers, chapters and finances.

Article 4 — Ideology, National Character and Independence

  1. Ideology: the Party holds that elected office is a mandate to deliver predetermined development objectives for the jurisdiction represented ("Governance by Objective"). Every candidate and every elected representative of the Party is bound by the Governance Mandate under Article 20 and the sanctions under Article 21.
  2. National character: membership shall be open to every Nigerian citizen who meets Article 5, without regard to ethnicity, religion, place of origin, gender, disability or social background. The Party's executive organs shall reflect the federal character of Nigeria. This Constitution and the Party's manifesto shall be registered with INEC.
  3. Funds shall come only from lawful sources, within the limits and disclosure rules of the Electoral Act, and shall not include funds from foreign sources or any source the law prohibits. Annual returns and accounts shall be filed with INEC as the law requires.
  4. The Party shall not maintain any armed, uniformed or militia-style group, and shall not use or permit violence, intimidation, vote-buying or hate speech in its name.
  5. Independence of assessment: the Party's governance assessment and mandate-audit functions shall be kept independent of fundraising, campaigning and mobilisation, shall assess office holders of every party by the same published methodology, and shall not be altered to favour or disadvantage any person or party, including the Party's own members.

Article 5 — Membership

  1. Membership is open to Nigerian citizens and lawful residents of Nigeria aged 18 and above who accept this Constitution, the ideology of the Party and the Code of Conduct, irrespective of ethnicity, religion, gender, disability or social background. No person may belong to the Party and another political party at the same time.
  2. Membership is voluntary and free by default. No member shall be required to disclose religion or biometric data for ordinary membership. The Party shall keep a register of members and make it available to INEC as the Electoral Act requires.
  3. Categories of membership shall include Ordinary Member, Ward Accountability Representative, Community Development Representative, Local Government Accountability Representative, State Volunteer, National Volunteer, Professional and Technical Volunteer, and Research and Policy Volunteer.
  4. Rights of members include to: participate in meetings and consultations of their chapter; vote and stand for office subject to this Constitution; access PGC's public reports and approved financial disclosures; receive notice and a fair hearing before any disciplinary action; and access, correct or delete their personal data in accordance with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.
  5. Obligations of members include to: act peacefully and lawfully; uphold the ideology of the Party and its Governance Mandate rules; submit only truthful information and evidence; and comply with the Code of Conduct.
  6. Membership ends by resignation, death, or termination under Article 23.

Article 6 — Organs of Governance

The organs of PGC Nigeria are: the National General Assembly; the Board of Trustees; the National Governing Council; the National Executive Secretariat; the National Technical and Research Council; the Independent Accountability and Ethics Committee; State Chapters; Local Government Accountability Chapters; and Ward Accountability Committees.

Article 7 — National General Assembly

  1. The National General Assembly is the supreme decision-making organ. It comprises delegates elected by each State Chapter and the FCT Chapter in proportion to verified active membership, together with members of the National Governing Council and Board of Trustees (who shall not vote on matters concerning their own conduct or remuneration).
  2. It shall meet at least once a year. An extraordinary session may be convened by the National Governing Council or at the written request of one-third of State Chapters.
  3. Its functions include adopting and amending this Constitution; electing members of the National Governing Council; receiving the annual report, audited accounts and national governance report; and ratifying the appointment of Trustees.
  4. The quorum is one-half of registered delegates representing at least two-thirds of the States and the FCT.

Article 8 — Board of Trustees

  1. The Board of Trustees shall comprise not fewer than five and not more than nine persons of proven integrity, including representation from at least four geopolitical zones and at least one-third women.
  2. Trustees hold the property of PGC Nigeria in trust, ensure compliance with this Constitution and the law, and safeguard institutional independence. Trustees shall not hold executive office in the National Governing Council at the same time.
  3. Trustees serve a term of four years, renewable once. A Trustee may be removed by a two-thirds vote of the National General Assembly after notice and a hearing.
  4. The Board shall act by majority; a quorum is a simple majority of Trustees in office.

Article 9 — National Governing Council

  1. The National Governing Council comprises the National President; Deputy National President; National Secretary; Deputy National Secretary; National Treasurer; National Financial Secretary; National Organising Secretary; National Director of Research and Governance Assessment; National Director of Membership and Community Engagement; National Director of Communications and Public Affairs; National Director of Digital Infrastructure; National Director of Legal Affairs and Institutional Compliance; and one representative from each of the six geopolitical zones. The General Assembly may approve additional seats to ensure inclusiveness.
  2. The Council directs policy and programmes between sessions of the General Assembly, approves annual budgets for ratification, and supervises the Secretariat.
  3. Members are elected by the National General Assembly for a term of three years, renewable once in the same office.

Article 10 — National Executive Secretariat

  1. The Secretariat, headed by an Executive Secretary appointed by the National Governing Council on merit, implements decisions and manages day-to-day operations, staff and records.
  2. It shall include departments for Governance Research; Project Monitoring; Finance and Budget Analysis; Membership and Mobilisation; Legal and Institutional Affairs; Digital Technology; Communications; Monitoring and Evaluation; and Ethics and Integrity (the last reporting functionally to the Independent Accountability and Ethics Committee).
  3. Staff may receive reasonable salaries approved through the budget. Staff shall not hold elective office in PGC Nigeria.

Article 11 — State Chapters

  1. There shall be a Chapter in each of the 36 States and the Federal Capital Territory, established on approval of the National Governing Council.
  2. Each State Chapter is led by a State Executive comprising a State Coordinator, Deputy Coordinator, Secretary, Treasurer, Membership Officer, Research and Governance Assessment Officer, Communications Officer, and Legal and Compliance Liaison, elected by the State Chapter assembly for three years, renewable once.
  3. State Chapters coordinate Local Government Chapters, submit quarterly activity reports, and conduct the six-monthly state governance review.

Article 12 — Local Government Accountability Chapters

  1. A Chapter may be established in each Local Government Area and FCT Area Council on approval of the State Chapter, with notice to the National Secretariat.
  2. Each Chapter coordinates community consultations, development priority registers, project monitoring, citizen reporting, quarterly assessments and government engagement in its area, and meets at least monthly.
  3. Its officers are elected by its members for three years, renewable once.

Article 13 — Ward Accountability Committees

  1. Ward Accountability Committees are the grassroots operational units. They identify local needs, collect evidence, monitor projects weekly, organise community meetings and submit quarterly reports to their Local Government Chapter.
  2. A Committee shall have not fewer than five members resident in the ward and is led by a Ward Coordinator elected by its members.

Article 14 — National Technical and Research Council

  1. The Council comprises volunteer professionals in fields including public administration, economics, law, engineering, architecture, accounting, auditing, data science, information technology, public health, education, agriculture, urban planning and development studies.
  2. It advises on project feasibility, designs performance indicators, analyses budgets, supports verification, and recommends the assessment methodology for approval by the National Governing Council.
  3. Members shall declare conflicts of interest and recuse themselves from any assessment in which they have a personal, financial or political interest.

Article 15 — Independent Accountability and Ethics Committee

  1. The Committee comprises five persons of integrity, at least two of whom are legal practitioners, appointed by the Board of Trustees and ratified by the National General Assembly. No member may simultaneously hold office in the National Governing Council or a Chapter executive.
  2. It receives and investigates complaints of financial misconduct, abuse of authority, political interference or capture, manipulation of assessments, harassment, discrimination, privacy violations, misuse of assets and conflicts of interest, and protects whistleblowers.
  3. It acts independently, reports annually to the General Assembly, and may refer matters to law-enforcement or regulatory authorities.
  4. Members serve a single term of four years.

Article 16 — Tenure and Organisational Elections

  1. All elective offices have a term of three years, renewable once in the same office, unless this Constitution provides otherwise. No person shall hold the same office for more than two consecutive terms.
  2. Elections shall be conducted by an Electoral Committee appointed for each election, whose members shall not be candidates. Notice of elections, eligibility criteria and the voting register shall be published in advance.
  3. Voting shall be by secret ballot, in person or through a secure electronic system approved by the National Governing Council.
  4. Disputes shall be lodged within seven days of the declaration of results and determined by an Election Appeals Panel before handover.
  5. The founder of PGC Nigeria shall have no permanent, hereditary or reserved position. Any role of the founder shall be determined by this Constitution as adopted and by founding resolutions of the inaugural General Assembly, and shall be subject to the same term limits and procedures as any other office.
  6. Outgoing officers shall complete a documented handover of records, assets and pending matters within thirty days.

Article 17 — Finance and Asset Management

  1. Funds may come from voluntary donations, grants, lawful fundraising and other lawful sources consistent with Article 4 and the Electoral Act. Candidate campaign funds shall be recorded separately from Party funds.
  2. No individual shall have control over more than one of: fundraising, payment approval, disbursement and reconciliation of the same transaction.
  3. All expenditure shall be within an approved budget, supported by documents and receipts, and recorded in the financial records.
  4. Accounts shall be audited annually by an independent external auditor and the approved financial statements published.
  5. Donor restrictions shall be honoured and recorded. Private donor information shall not be published without consent.
  6. No profit, income or property of PGC Nigeria shall be distributed to members, Trustees or officers, except reasonable remuneration for services approved through the budget and reimbursement of approved expenses.

Article 18 — Governance by Objective

  1. Every candidate of the Party shall seek office on a Governance Mandate: a published list of predetermined development projects ("Mandate Projects") for the jurisdiction that the office represents.
  2. A project is predetermined only if it was recorded in the Party's public Development Register, with its community source, before the aspirant registered in the Candidate Register. A project added after an aspirant registers may not be used in that aspirant's Mandate.
  3. The Development Register is built from ward, Local Government and State consultations conducted by the Party's Chapters. State Chapters shall publish an Objectives Schedule for each State, and the National Governing Council one for federal offices.
  4. The Party's manifesto at every level shall consist of its candidates' Mandates and the Objectives Schedules from which they are drawn.
  5. A Mandate does not replace constitutional responsibilities, statutory duties or approved budgets. The Party shall not require or promise what an office has no lawful power to do, and Mandate Projects shall be delivered through lawful appropriation, procurement and oversight processes.

Article 19 — Candidate Eligibility and Selection

  1. A person may seek the Party's ticket only if the person: is a member in good standing for not less than [six] months; meets every qualification for the office in the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and the Electoral Act; has accepted the Candidate Code and the Party Rules and Regulations; has declared assets and interests as the Rules require; and is not subject to an unresolved finding by the Independent Accountability and Ethics Committee.
  2. An aspirant shall register in the Candidate Register and shall be screened by the Screening Committee for the relevant level. No aspirant shall be cleared unless the aspirant has signed a Governance Mandate that satisfies Article 20.
  3. Primaries shall be conducted in accordance with the Electoral Act and notified to INEC. The Party shall not impose candidates. The Mandates of all cleared aspirants shall be published before any primary is held.
  4. Fees and forms shall be reasonable and published, and shall be reduced or waived for women, young persons and persons with disabilities.
  5. Particulars of a nominated candidate shall be submitted to INEC as the law requires, and the candidate's Mandate summary shall be published with the candidate's profile.

Article 20 — The Governance Mandate

  1. A Governance Mandate shall contain: at least [three] Mandate Projects from the Development Register within the candidate's jurisdiction; a target completion date for each, falling within the tenure of the office; acceptance of the Mandate Rules; and a pledge to publish quarterly delivery reports.
  2. Jurisdiction: for the office of President, projects anywhere in the Federation; for Governor, Senator, Member of the House of Representatives and Member of a State House of Assembly, projects in the State; for Local Government Chairperson and Councillor, projects in the Local Government Area or Area Council.
  3. A Mandate becomes binding on signature and shall be published. After signature no Mandate Project shall be removed or substituted except by the Mandate Committee, for lawful impossibility (such as a court order, a change in law or prior delivery by another body), with the reasons published.
  4. A Mandate Committee at each level of the Party, advised by the National Technical and Research Council, shall review each Mandate for legality, costing, feasibility and relevance, and may require it to be revised before screening.
  5. A Mandate is a political commitment between the candidate, the Party and the people of the jurisdiction. It does not require any person to act unlawfully. The Party shall not demand any undated resignation letter, security instrument or other document intended to remove a person from lawful office, and any such demand is void.

Article 21 — Delivery, Reporting and Sanctions

  1. A Mandate Project counts as "Delivered" only when its Development Register entry is independently verified. "Completed" means reported complete and awaiting verification. An allocation or release of funds is never proof of delivery.
  2. Every office holder who was sponsored by the Party shall publish a delivery report within [thirty] days of each quarter end. The independent assessors shall publish a Mandate Audit, subject to a right of reply.
  3. At the midpoint of a tenure, the Mandate Audit shall report on all Mandate Projects due by then. If less than [half] of them are Delivered or demonstrably on schedule, a Public Delivery Warning shall be issued.
  4. After notice and a fair hearing under Article 23, the following sanctions may be imposed for failure to deliver without good cause, in order of severity: delivery caution; public censure with a recovery plan and deadlines; suspension of Party privileges and support; denial of the Party's ticket for any later term; and suspension or termination of membership.
  5. No failure shall be sanctioned if the office holder shows, with documents, that non-delivery was caused by matters beyond the office holder's control (such as non-release of appropriated funds, a court order, or force majeure) and that the office holder diligently pursued delivery. The Mandate Committee shall decide, and publish its reasons.
  6. No sanction under this Constitution removes any person from public office, and the Party shall not purport to do so. Where the Constitution allows citizens to petition for recall of an elected member of a legislature, the Party shall support civic education on that process but shall not itself abuse it.
  7. Every Mandate, delivery report, Mandate Audit, warning and sanction shall be published on the Party's public platform.

Article 22 — Quarterly Governance Assessment

  1. The Party shall publish quarterly assessments of elected offices of every party using a published methodology approved by the National Governing Council on the advice of the Technical and Research Council.
  2. Assessments shall cover only responsibilities legally attributable to each office, distinguish citizen allegations, official claims, verified findings and independent assessments, and record "Not Yet Rated" where evidence is insufficient.
  3. Every assessed office holder shall have a right of reply, and any person may request a correction or appeal an assessment.
  4. The Party's own office holders shall be assessed by the same methodology and the same independent assessors as all others. Assessments are civic evaluations and shall not be presented as criminal findings, judicial decisions or statutory audits.
  5. No officer may alter an assessment to favour or disadvantage any person or party.

Article 23 — Discipline and Code of Conduct

  1. The National Governing Council shall adopt a Code of Conduct for members and officers.
  2. No member or officer shall be disciplined without written notice of the allegations, access to the evidence relied on, a reasonable opportunity (of not less than fourteen days) to respond, and a reasoned decision.
  3. Sanctions are, in order of severity: caution, reprimand, suspension from office, removal from office, suspension of membership and termination of membership.
  4. Any person sanctioned may appeal within twenty-one days to a panel not previously involved in the matter. Internal appeal does not limit any lawful external remedy.

Article 24 — Conflict Resolution

  1. Disputes between members, officers or organs shall first be resolved by dialogue and, failing that, mediation by a person agreed by the parties or appointed by the Ethics Committee.
  2. Unresolved disputes may be referred to arbitration under the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023, without limiting any lawful right to seek redress in court.

Article 25 — Amendment of the Constitution

  1. A proposal to amend this Constitution may be made by the National Governing Council, the Board of Trustees, or one-quarter of State Chapters.
  2. The text of a proposed amendment shall be circulated to all Chapters at least sixty days before the General Assembly at which it is considered.
  3. An amendment requires a two-thirds majority of delegates present and voting, and takes effect upon any registration required by law.
  4. Each amendment shall be recorded with its date, resolution reference and the prior text.

Article 26 — Dissolution

  1. The Party may be dissolved or merged with another party by a resolution passed by three-quarters of delegates at two successive General Assemblies held not less than three months apart, or as required by law.
  2. On dissolution, after settlement of liabilities, remaining assets shall not be distributed to members, Trustees or officers, but transferred to one or more non-profit organisations with similar objectives, as the General Assembly determines and the law permits.

Article 27 — Interpretation and Supremacy

  1. This Constitution is subject to the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended) and applicable laws. Any provision inconsistent with them is void to the extent of the inconsistency.
  2. Within PGC Nigeria, this Constitution prevails over any rule, policy or decision of any organ.
  3. Questions of interpretation shall be determined by the Board of Trustees on the advice of the National Director of Legal Affairs, subject to review by the General Assembly.

Article 28 — Transitional Provisions

  1. Until the inaugural National General Assembly, an Interim Steering Committee of not fewer than seven members drawn from at least four geopolitical zones shall prepare the application for registration with INEC, convene the inaugural Assembly within twelve months and administer the organisation. Its members shall not be eligible to serve more than one term in any office on that basis alone.
  2. Interim appointments to Chapters shall lapse at the first Chapter elections, which shall be held within eighteen months of the establishment of each Chapter.
  3. This Constitution takes effect on adoption by the inaugural National General Assembly. Until then it is a Proposed Draft for consultation.
